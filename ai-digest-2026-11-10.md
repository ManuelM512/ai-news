## 9. Research — RSIGym: Agent Environment for Recursive Self-Improvement

**Source:** [arXiv 2610.10310](https://www.roboticscenter.ai/research/papers/rsigym-a-flexible-environment-for-recursive-self-improvement-2610)

RSIGym is a new agent-native benchmark environment that exposes training, evaluation, and sandboxing services with shared budget controls to study recursive self-improvement (RSI) in LLM agents. Claude Opus 5 achieved the highest RSI-Index of 0.4809 under a $500 budget.

**Why it matters:** Standardized RSI evaluation infrastructure is essential for measuring whether agents genuinely improve their own code/training over iterations—a key step toward understanding and governing increasingly autonomous AI systems.

## 10. Research — AI4AI-Bench: Agents Rewriting Their Own Training Algorithms

**Source:** [arXiv 2608.20318](https://deeplearn.org/arxiv/809834/ai4ai-bench:-benchmarking-llm-agents-in-algorithmic-design-for-recursive-self-improvement) · [Codex write-up](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/08/26/ai4ai-bench-coding-agents-recursive-self-improvement-training-algorithm-design-codex-cli/)

AI4AI-Bench tasks coding agents with rewriting a frozen repository's training algorithm within a four-hour, single-GPU budget; a hidden evaluator scores the rewritten code. The best system among 6 tested reached a score of 0.250 (mean 0.166), highlighting the difficulty of autonomous algorithm design.

**Why it matters:** As agentic coding systems grow more capable, rigorous benchmarks for self-directed algorithmic improvement are critical for calibrating trust and risk in AI-assisted research workflows.
