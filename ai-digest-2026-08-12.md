## New Model

### OpenAI Ships GPT-5.6-Cyber, Its First "Offense-Grade" Cybersecurity Model
**Source:** [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonmarkman/2026/08/11/openai-ships-gpt-56-cyber-its-first-offense-grade-hacking-model/) · [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/10/openai-gpt-astra-restrictions-safety-hacking-defenders) · [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/openai-daybreak-blue-red-gpt-cyber/)

OpenAI launched **GPT-5.6-Cyber** on August 10, a purpose-trained cybersecurity model that completes 95% of requests on OpenAI's internal Advanced Cybersecurity Completion Rate benchmark — versus 1.5% for the general-purpose GPT-5.6 Sol. It is available only via the new **Daybreak Red** access tier, reserved for vetted organizations doing authorized vulnerability research, exploit validation, and security testing. A lower tier, **Daybreak Blue**, gives approved defenders access to frontier models with system-level safeguards relaxed. OpenAI used the model internally to find two previously unknown V8 (Chrome) vulnerabilities, now patched by Google as CVE-2026-15903.

**Why it matters:** A frontier-grade model purpose-built and policy-gated for offensive security is a significant shift — it acknowledges that dual-use hacking capability at this level exists and tries to control distribution through vetted tiers rather than across-the-board refusals. The real-world CVE discovery validates the approach and sets a precedent for how AI labs might release increasingly dangerous capabilities responsibly.

## New Agent Tool

### Microsoft Agent Framework Harness and Hosted Agents Reach General Availability
**Source:** [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/agent-framework-harness-ga/) · [Microsoft Dev Blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/agent-framework/the-microsoft-agent-framework-harness-is-now-released/) · [Nerd Level Tech](https://nerdleveltech.com/agent-harness-ga-microsoft-agent-framework)

Microsoft's **Agent Harness** and **Foundry Hosted Agents** reached general availability in August 2026, completing the platform's evolution from an SDK for building agents to a governed runtime for running them in production. The Harness provides the execution layer between model reasoning and real-world action: shell and filesystem access, human-in-the-loop approval flows, and context management across long-running sessions. Hosted Agents are now available across 31 Azure regions. A v1.13.0 update also shipped this week, adding richer Foundry hosting, reusable session stores, and expanded telemetry and observability.

**Why it matters:** GA status transforms the Agent Framework from an experimental toolkit (the merged AutoGen + Semantic Kernel) into enterprise-grade infrastructure. The Harness's approval-flow primitives and the 31-region Hosted Agents footprint give enterprise teams the governance and deployment guarantees they need to run agents in regulated environments — a gap that open-source frameworks still struggle to fill.

## New Model

### Seedance 2.5 Developer API Opens: 30-Second Audio-Video Generation With 30 References
**Source:** [EvoLink](https://evolink.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-api-status) · [HowAIWorks](https://howaiworks.ai/models/seedance) · [Hedra](https://www.hedra.com/models/video/bytedance/seedance-25)

ByteDance opened the public developer API for **Seedance 2.5** on August 7, following the model's creator-tier release on July 31. The model generates up to 30 seconds of audio-video in a single pass and accepts up to 30 image, 10 video, and 10 audio reference inputs simultaneously, plus timestamp-level editing. It is currently rolling out to subscribers across Europe, Asia, the Middle East, and South America via CapCut's Dreamina interface; no U.S. launch date has been announced.

**Why it matters:** The ability to weave dozens of references into a single coherent video generation pass — audio, video, and image together — raises the bar for multi-modal video tools. Opening the API shortly after the creator release signals ByteDance is moving to capture developer workflows before competitors can ship comparable capabilities.

## Other

### NAVER, NVIDIA, and Brookfield Triple Korea's Sovereign AI Factory to 200 MW in $10B Deal
**Source:** [NVIDIA Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/naver-nvidia-and-brookfield-to-expand-koreas-national-ai-factory-infrastructure-buildout) · [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321630/20260726/naver-nvidia-triple-koreas-ai-factory-200-megawatts-10-billion-deal-brookfield.htm) · [Data Centre Magazine](https://datacentremagazine.com/news/how-naver-nvidia-brookfield-plan-to-build-a-200mw-ai-hub)

NAVER, NVIDIA, and Brookfield Asset Management announced a $10 billion deal to expand Korea's national AI factory infrastructure from 55 MW to **200 MW** by 2028 at NAVER's GAK Sejong hyperscale data center. Brookfield is funding up to $9 billion; NVIDIA is investing $1 billion and deploying its DSX AI Factory platform; NAVER is contributing the remainder. The long-term target is 1 gigawatt of sovereign AI compute for Korea.

**Why it matters:** Sovereign AI — nationally controlled compute under a country's own data laws — is becoming a distinct procurement category. A $10B commitment from a credible Brookfield + NVIDIA combination gives this deal structural weight beyond a press release, and the 1-GW ambition signals that mid-tier economies are moving to secure frontier compute independence rather than relying solely on US hyperscaler capacity.

### U.S. Federal Government Commits $5B Across 15+ Agencies to Embed AI in Scientific Research
**Source:** [Mintz — AI: The Washington Report, August 2026](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54941/2026-08-07-ai-washington-report-august-2026-edition)

The administration announced a commitment of over **$5 billion across 15+ federal agencies** to integrate AI into national scientific research programs — covering life sciences, energy, defense, and semiconductor domains. The commitment creates new federal demand for AI infrastructure and AI-native research tooling, and signals a significant expansion of government-as-buyer in the AI market.

**Why it matters:** Federal science budgets that include AI-by-default requirements will reshape procurement for cloud providers, AI labs, and specialized research tooling vendors. The life sciences and energy components are particularly notable — both are domains where AI inference at scale (drug discovery, materials modeling) is already outpacing conventional compute procurement.
