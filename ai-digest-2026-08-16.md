## New Model

### 1. Z.ai Ships GLM-5.3 — Open-Weights Coding SOTA via Post-Training Alone
**Source:** [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/14/z-ai-ships-glm-5-3-without-retraining-the-base-model-better-at-complex-coding-and-long-horizon-tasks/) · [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/14/z-ai-debuts-glm-5-3-long-horizon-coding-cybersecurity-upgrades/) · [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/z-ai-launches-glm-5-3-with-frontier-coding-and-a-cyber-capability-that-outgrew-its-training/)

Z.ai released GLM-5.3 on August 14, reusing the 743B-parameter MoE base from GLM-5.2 and deriving all improvements from scaled post-training. The result: a 50% jump on Z.ai's Code Bench, top ranking on CyberGym (84.5%), and a doubling of exploit-detection capability over GLM-5.2. Weights are not yet public — Z.ai says it will release them in approximately two weeks pending safety hardening. API and coding-plan access is live now.

**Why it matters:** GLM-5.3 is the clearest proof yet that post-training alone — without touching the base model — can push a model from competitive to SOTA in specialized domains. The unexpectedly fast cybersecurity gains during scale-up are also drawing scrutiny from researchers monitoring dual-use risks in frontier open-weight models.

### 2. Meta Open-Sources Muse Glimmer, a 30B On-Device Agent Model
**Source:** [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/meta-returns-to-open-source-with-muse-glimmer-an-apache-2-0-licensed-30b-parameter-ai-model-optimized-for-agents-available-now) · [Meta AI Research](https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-glimmer-open-agentic-model) · [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Meta-Muse-Glimmer)

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Glimmer on August 10 under Apache 2.0 — a 29.6B-parameter dense multimodal model engineered for local execution on consumer hardware. With 4-bit quantization the model fits in 18–20 GB of VRAM, enabling single-GPU deployment on 24 GB or 32 GB cards. It is optimized for agentic tool use, coding, schedule management, file organization, and LLM-as-judge evaluations, with a 131K context window and 100+ language support.

**Why it matters:** Muse Glimmer is Meta's attempt to establish a competitive open-weight baseline for on-device agents at a time when most frontier agentic models require cloud endpoints. Apache 2.0 licensing and llama.cpp/MLX/ExecuTorch integration targets the developer community most likely to build local-first AI tooling.

## New Framework

### 3. Google Showcases HEIR — Open-Source Compiler for Private AI Inference on Encrypted Data
**Source:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/security/how-google-is-making-private-ai-practical-with-homomorphic-encryption/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/google/heir) · [HEIR Project](https://heir.dev/)

Google highlighted HEIR (Homomorphic Encryption Intermediate Representation) as a production-ready addition to its Private Computing Toolkit on August 13. Built on the MLIR compiler framework, HEIR converts pretrained models into forms that run inference on fully encrypted inputs — the server processes ciphertext and never sees underlying data. It supports OpenFHE and Lattigo backends and targets application developers, compiler engineers, and cryptography researchers.

**Why it matters:** Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) has long been too slow and complex to use with real ML workloads. HEIR's compiler-based approach, combined with its open-source release, lowers the barrier for building privacy-preserving AI services — particularly relevant for healthcare, finance, and legal applications where data sovereignty is non-negotiable.

### 5. Open Secure AI Alliance Proposes SAFE Incident-Sharing Framework at Black Hat
**Source:** [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/open-secure-ai-alliance-contributions/) · [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/04/open-secure-ai-alliance-proposes-safe-guidelines-membership-tops-120/) · [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/cybersecurity-alliance-drafts-safe-guidelines-for-sharing-ai-incident-data/amp/)

The Open Secure AI Alliance (now 120+ member organizations) published a Linux Foundation Request for Comments on Shared AI Findings Exchange (SAFE) — a framework requiring members to notify affected customers within 72 hours of a "credible data exposure" and report incidents to the exchange within four business days. Lead contributors include NVIDIA, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Hugging Face, and Red Hat.

**Why it matters:** Agentic systems introduce a new class of security incident where the AI itself is the threat actor (accidentally or otherwise). SAFE creates a near-miss sharing mechanism analogous to aviation's safety reporting culture — confidential disclosure in exchange for ecosystem-wide protection. Its adoption will be a key test of whether the AI industry can self-regulate on security.

### 6. Manus Regains Independence as China Forces Meta to Unwind $2 Billion Acquisition
**Source:** [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/manus-to-resume-independent-operations-in-unwind-of-meta-deal) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/11/manus-china-meta-acquisition.html) · [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/08/15/facebook-parent-meta-to-unwind-us2-billion-manus-ai-deal-after-beijing-block)

Manus confirmed on August 11 that it will return to independent operations after China's NDRC ordered Meta to unwind its $2 billion acquisition — completed December 29, 2025 — citing technology-export and foreign-investment regulations. Manus co-founders were summoned to Beijing in March and barred from leaving the country during the review. User data generated after the acquisition date will be deleted August 23–24, with restoration beginning August 25.

**Why it matters:** The forced unwind is the highest-profile example of China treating an AI agent company as a strategic asset subject to national review. It signals that cross-border acquisitions of agentic AI companies will face new geopolitical friction — especially where the target has user data and agentic capabilities touching sensitive workflows.

### 7. tl;dv AI Notetaker Exposed 181,874 Meeting Records — Flaw Unreported for 7 Months
**Source:** [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/application-security/ai-notetaker-spy-government-corporate-video-calls) · [explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/tldv-firestore-breach-181000-meetings-exposed-2026) · [Netizen Blog](https://blog.netizen.net/2026/08/04/inside-the-tldv-flaw-that-exposed-live-government-and-corporate-meetings/)

A missing Firestore tenant-isolation rule in tl;dv — a popular AI meeting notetaker for Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams — left 181,874 meeting records queryable by any authenticated user. Records spanned 84,312 users across 35,003 domains, including .gov endpoints from 23 countries. Any exposed conference ID could be used to join a live meeting. The flaw was responsibly disclosed in January 2026 but remained unpatched for over six months.

**Why it matters:** AI notetakers have quietly become a high-value target: they aggregate meeting content, participant data, and live conference IDs across enterprise and government customers. This breach underlines the systemic risk of SaaS AI tools that store sensitive real-time data without rigorous multi-tenancy security, particularly as these services expand to government and regulated industries.
