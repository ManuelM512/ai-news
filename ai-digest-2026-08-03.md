### 2. DeepSeek V4-Flash 0731 Officially Released — Beats Pro Model on Agent Benchmarks
**Source:** [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-08-01/deepseek-releases-official-v4-flash-model-as-chinas-ai-race-intensifies-102470292.html) · [Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/ResterChed/deepseek-v4-flash-official-release) · [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/deepseek-v4-flash-0731-official-release-agent-benchmarks)

DeepSeek officially released V4-Flash (build 0731) on July 31, exiting the preview it had been in since April. The model retains its 284B/13B MoE architecture; all performance gains come from post-training rather than architectural changes. It scored 82.7% on Terminal-Bench, outperforming DeepSeek's own 1.6T V4-Pro on agent-specific benchmarks. Pricing stays at $0.14 per million input tokens (cache miss) / $0.0028 (cache hit) / $0.28 output — unchanged from preview. The V4-Pro official release is expected in early August.

**Why it matters:** An agent-benchmark win over a model with 5× more parameters, at $0.14/M input, cements V4-Flash as the most cost-efficient option for autonomous agent pipelines. The 98%-off cache-hit pricing is particularly significant for agents that repeatedly reference the same context.

## Other (Policy)

### 3. EU AI Act Transparency and GPAI Enforcement Goes Live
**Source:** [European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1714) · [AI Journal](https://aijourn.com/eu-ai-act-enforcement-expands-on-2-august-2026-are-your-ai-systems-compliant/) · [Olakai](https://olakai.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-enforcement-august-2026/)

On August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office and national authorities began enforcing the EU AI Act's transparency obligations and general-purpose AI (GPAI) provisions. Chatbots must now disclose they are AI to users; deepfakes and AI-generated content must carry visible labels and machine-readable watermarks; and the AI Office now has supervisory and fining authority over GPAI model providers. High-risk system rules were pushed to December 2027–August 2028 by the Digital Omnibus, but prohibitions and transparency requirements are active now.

**Why it matters:** This is the world's most comprehensive AI law moving from text to enforcement. It directly affects every major AI lab with EU users, requiring product changes for chatbots and content pipelines and setting a precedent for AI regulation globally.

### 4. EU Launches Call for Seven AI Gigafactories — €30B+ Investment Unlocked
**Source:** [Silicon Republic](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/business/eu-launches-call-to-build-seven-ai-gigafactories) · [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/eu-launches-ai-gigafactories-call-boost-europes-computing-capacity-and-unlock-more-eu30-billion) · [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/07/30/eu-opens-call-for-seven-gigafactories-to-train-next-generation-ai-technologies)

The EU opened a competitive call for tenders to build up to seven AI Gigafactories across Europe, targeting over €30 billion in combined EU, national, and private investment. Each facility will provide large-scale compute infrastructure for training, inference, and fine-tuning frontier AI models, accessible to startups, academia, and public institutions. Bids are due November 12; awards will be announced in early 2027. Ten member states — including Germany, France, Italy, and Poland — have expressed interest in hosting a facility.

**Why it matters:** With the US and China dominating frontier compute, this is Europe's most concrete bet on AI sovereignty. Broad academic and SME access could accelerate open-source and specialized European AI models at a scale not previously possible inside the EU.

## Other (Legal)

### 5. Suno Loses Landmark AI Music Copyright Case to GEMA in Germany
**Source:** [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/suno-loses-ai-lawsuit-gema-1236825010/) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/german-court-rules-ai-music-generator-suno-violated-copyrights-rejects-fair-use-defense/) · [Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/03/339222.htm)

Munich's 42nd Civil Chamber ruled on July 31 that AI music generator Suno infringed copyrights held by German collecting society GEMA, rejecting Suno's fair-use defense. The court found that Suno permanently stored recognizable versions of protected works in its model parameters — treating memorization as infringement under German law. Suno must stop reproducing the six works cited, disclose revenues from their unlicensed use, and pay yet-to-be-quantified damages. Suno said it would evaluate all options including an appeal.

**Why it matters:** This is Europe's first court ruling on AI music copyright and sets a precedent that training-time memorization equals infringement — a far stricter standard than US approaches. If upheld on appeal, it could force AI music generators to license every work or retrain without memorization.

## Research

### 6. OpenAI Field Report: Coding Agents Achieve Up to 60× Speedup on Scientific Software
**Source:** [OpenAI Blog](https://openai.com/index/scientific-computing-agentic-ai/) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-coding-agents-can-modernize-research-software-but-cant-judge-if-the-science-is-right/) · [OpenAI on X](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2082152074071228702)

OpenAI and academic partners published a field report showing AI coding agents can modernize neglected scientific software, with measured speedups up to 60×. The standout case: a genomics quality-control pipeline (RustQC) went from 15 hours 34 minutes to 14 minutes 54 seconds. Another project (HelixForge) replaced a GPU-unaware data generation tool, achieving a 59.6× speedup. The report notes a critical limitation: agents executed well-defined coding tasks reliably but could not judge scientific correctness — researchers must still set the questions, verify results, and own long-term maintenance.

**Why it matters:** 60× runtime improvements on real research pipelines is a concrete productivity signal beyond synthetic benchmarks. The acknowledged limitation — agents can code but not reason about domain correctness — is important guidance for labs and researchers considering how deeply to trust agentic refactoring.

### 7. AT&T and Verizon Cut Thousands More Jobs as AI Automation Expands
**Source:** [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/at-t-and-verizon-cut-thousands-more-jobs-as-ai-backlash-grows) · [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320972/20260719/verizon-cuts-16600-jobs-nine-months-its-ai-stack-nears-completion.htm) · [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/employment/verizon-ceo-cuts-to-the-chase-new-layoffs-ai-future)

Verizon has cut over 16,600 positions since October 2025 as CEO Dan Schulman completes an AI-first restructuring; a new WARN filing shows 121 more roles ending August 7 at its Basking Ridge HQ. Schulman stated publicly that AI will replace "a large percentage" of customer service. AT&T has quietly eliminated an additional 2,100 jobs in 2026, following 8,000 cuts in 2025. Broader industry trackers show AI-driven layoffs accelerating across retail, financial services, and logistics as agentic customer-service and workflow-automation deployments mature.

**Why it matters:** Telecom has some of the most automatable customer-service operations, making it a leading indicator of AI's employment impact at scale. The pace of cuts in 2026 — and executives' public attribution to AI — marks a shift from "AI as productivity tool" to "AI as workforce reduction driver" entering mainstream corporate strategy.
