## New Model

### 1. OpenAI Unveils "Astra" — Next Major Model Family Solves 10 Decade-Old Math Problems
**Source:** [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-announces-its-next-major-model-astra-by-dropping-ten-previously-unsolved-math-solutions/) · [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/node/8675) · [Byte Iota](https://byteiota.com/openai-astra-multi-agent-model/)

OpenAI researcher Noam Brown announced on August 1 that an internal version of "Astra" — the company's next major model family — solved ten open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science that had seen no progress for at least a decade, spanning group theory, coding theory, quantum complexity, and high-dimensional geometry. Astra is designed around multi-agent collaboration: several coordinating agents can plan, revise, delegate, and work in parallel for hours or even days on research, coding, and scientific tasks. CEO Sam Altman has already previewed it for US senators and regulators; no public release date has been set, and whether it ships under the GPT-5.x or GPT-6 brand is still undecided. All Astra versions will go through the new government pre-release review process established by EO 14409.

**Why it matters:** Astra represents a generational shift in OpenAI's architecture — away from single-shot queries toward long-horizon, collaborative agent systems. Solving decade-old math problems in the announcement is a credibility marker that positions it as a step-change above GPT-5.x.

## New Framework

### 2. MCP 2026-07-28 Specification Ships — Protocol Goes Fully Stateless
**Source:** [MCP Blog — Spec Post](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/) · [MCP Blog — RC Post](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/) · [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/appsonazureblog/mcp-just-went-stateless-%E2%80%94-what-the-2026-spec-changes-about-scaling-on-app-servic/4530222)

The final Model Context Protocol specification (2026-07-28) shipped on July 28, marking the biggest revision to MCP since its launch in late 2024. The headline change: the initialize/initialized handshake and the `Mcp-Session-Id` header are gone. Protocol version, client info, and capabilities now travel inline on every request via a `_meta` field, making the protocol fully stateless. Any server instance can now serve any request, meaning remote MCP servers can run behind a plain round-robin load balancer with no sticky sessions or shared session store. The RC also adds Multi Round-Trip Requests, cacheable list results, authorization hardening, and a formal extensions framework. All four Tier-1 SDKs shipped support by publication day.

**Why it matters:** Stateless MCP unlocks horizontal cloud deployments without the session-affinity hacks that made production MCP servers expensive and fragile. This directly unblocks enterprise-scale agentic infrastructure built on MCP.

## New Agent Tool

### 3. Gemini 3.5 Flash Computer Use Tool Enters Public Preview
**Source:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-computer-use-gemini-3-5-flash/) · [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-gemini-3-5-flash-computer-use-built-in-tool) · [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/gemini-3-5-flash-computer-use-agent-automation-2026)

Google added computer use as a native built-in tool inside the production Gemini 3.5 Flash model (not a separate computer-use-only model), available in public preview through the Gemini API and Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform. Agents can now see screens, click, type, and scroll across browsers, mobile devices, and desktops in a single inference pass — perception and action are collapsed together. The tool logs are now visible in the AI Studio dashboard. Gemini 3.5 Flash with computer use scored 78.4% on OSWorld-Verified and includes configurable safety policies and built-in prompt-injection detection.

**Why it matters:** Integrating computer use directly into the main Flash model (rather than routing to a separate capability) lowers latency and cost for agentic automation pipelines, while 78.4% on OSWorld-Verified pushes the state of the art in real-world GUI control.

## Other (Policy)

### 4. California AI Transparency Act (SB 942) Becomes Operative Today
**Source:** [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/california-ai-transparency-law) · [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/californias-ongoing-ai-regulation-key-deadlines-arriving-2026-and-beyond) · [Regulations.AI](https://regulations.ai/regulations/california-sb-942-ai-transparency-act-2024)

California's AI Transparency Act — originally SB 942, amended by AB 853 and signed October 13, 2025 — officially became operative on August 2, 2026, deliberately aligned with the EU AI Act's high-risk enforcement date. Covered providers (generative AI systems with more than 1 million California monthly users) must now: embed C2PA-compatible content provenance in AI-generated images, video, and audio; offer a free public AI-detection tool; and allow users to add visible AI labels. AB 853 extended the scope to large online platforms (2M+ CA monthly users), generative AI hosting platforms that distribute model weights, and manufacturers of capture devices.

**Why it matters:** This is the first US state-level content provenance and transparency law to actually take effect. For every major AI lab and platform with significant US audiences, compliance is now legally required — and the August 2 date sets a concrete enforcement anchor for similar legislation in other states.

### 5. Unit 42: Threat Actor Weaponized DeepSeek + Open-Source Hermes Agent in 460-System Attack
**Source:** [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today) · [AI Flash Report](https://aiflashreport.com/model-releases.html)

Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 threat intelligence team published a report on August 2 detailing a Zhuhai-based threat actor tracked as "knaithe" who wired DeepSeek V4-Flash into the open-source Hermes Agent framework and directed the resulting autonomous agent via Telegram commands. The agent enumerated targets, sourced public exploits, and attacked 460+ internet-facing systems without human intervention for each step. This is the first publicly documented case of a frontier AI model embedded in a closed-loop offensive pipeline that reached triple-digit target counts.

**Why it matters:** This moves AI-assisted cyberattacks from proof-of-concept to active documented operations. The combination of a capable cheap API (DeepSeek V4-Flash at $0.14/M input tokens) and permissive open-source agent frameworks dramatically lowers the bar for autonomous offensive operations.

## Other (Industry)

### 6. Google Cancels AI Studio Mobile App Despite 800,000+ Preorders
**Source:** [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)

Google quietly canceled the planned AI Studio mobile app for iOS and Android, which had accumulated more than 800,000 preorders. No public explanation was given. Google AI Studio remains web-only; developer access to Gemini models on mobile is expected to flow through Gemini app integrations and the API rather than a dedicated developer application.

**Why it matters:** The cancellation highlights the ongoing gap between Google's model capabilities and its consumer product execution. With 800k preorders suggesting genuine developer demand, the decision also raises questions about Google's mobile-first strategy for AI tooling at a time when OpenAI's mobile apps are gaining traction.

### 7. Gemini 3.5 Pro Release Slips Again — Third Delay Since Google I/O
**Source:** [Find Skill AI](https://findskill.ai/blog/gemini-3-5-pro-release-date/) · [Presenc AI](https://presenc.ai/research/google-io-2026-gemini-3-5-pro-release-brief) · [OFOX AI](https://ofox.ai/blog/gemini-3-5-pro-release-date-expected-specs-2026/)

Gemini 3.5 Pro — announced at Google I/O on May 19, 2026 alongside the shipping Gemini 3.5 Flash — has slipped its public release date for the third time. The model entered internal testing immediately after I/O with a ~one-month public rollout target, but reportedly fell short of Google's internal quality gates on hallucination rates and real-world reliability. The latest slip puts the public launch window in "late August or September at the earliest," per developer-relations briefings. Google AI Studio already supports Computer Use on Gemini 3.5 Flash and has shipped 3.6 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite in the interim.

**Why it matters:** Three consecutive slips on a flagship model — while Flash variants continue to ship — signals that Google is prioritizing reliability over speed for its highest-capability offering, but also creates an opening for competitors to consolidate developer adoption while Pro remains unavailable.
