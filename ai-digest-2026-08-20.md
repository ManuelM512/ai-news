## 1. New Model · Google releases Gemini 3.7 Flash

**Source:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-3-7-flash/) · [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/13/gemini-3-7-flash-launch/)

Google launched **Gemini 3.7 Flash** on August 13 — its new primary workhorse model aimed at coding and agentic workflows. The model improves DeepSWE v1.1 benchmark performance from 49.0% to 65.3% over its predecessor and keeps the 1M-token context window with a 64K output limit. Pricing is set at $0.75/$3.75 per million input/output tokens through end of year.

**Why it matters:** As Google's main API model for software-engineering tasks, this directly competes with Claude Sonnet and GPT-4.1 Mini in the high-throughput agent tier — a segment that is rapidly becoming the default production target for enterprise AI.

**GLM-5.3** from Z.ai (Zhipu AI) shipped August 14 — a 743B MoE model (~40B active per token) post-trained on the same base as GLM-5.2. Standout numbers: Terminal-Bench 3.0 jumped from 4.6% to 28.3%, and CyberGym hit 84.5%. Open weights are expected around August 28 after a safety hardening window.

**Why it matters:** GLM-5.3 sets a new bar for open-weight models on offensive/defensive cybersecurity tasks. Once the weights land publicly, it will be the strongest freely available model for security-oriented agentic use cases.

## 4. Research · Cross-provider API flaw exposes hidden reasoning tokens

**Source:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/openai-anthropic-google-api-flaw-let.html) · [ExplainX](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/stealing-reasoning-traces-encrypted-cot-vulnerability-august-2026)

Researchers from ELLIS Institute Tübingen and Max Planck published findings (around August 10–12) showing OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google all used a **single global encryption key** to protect reasoning API tokens. By replaying encrypted reasoning blocks cross-session or into a weaker model in the same family, researchers decoded 315,320 reasoning blocks and recovered 182 credentials. The main extraction path has since been patched.

**Why it matters:** This is the first major demonstration that encrypted chain-of-thought mechanisms are not as isolated as assumed. It surfaces four realistic abuse classes — model distillation theft, cross-user data extraction, harmful-content exfiltration, and hidden prompt injection — that providers now need to design against explicitly.

## 5. Research · Google DeepMind maps four pathways from AGI to ASI

**Source:** [DeepMind Publications](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/239142/) · [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/google-deepmind-agi-to-asi-paper)

A 57-page DeepMind paper ("From AGI to ASI," arXiv:2606.12683, 14 authors) defines AGI as human-expert-level cognition and ASI as systems that consistently outperform the best human organizations at scale. It maps four pathways to ASI: **scaling, paradigm shifts, recursive self-improvement, and multi-agent collectives**, and identifies likely bottlenecks on each path. The paper crossed 54,000 views within days of posting.

**Why it matters:** This is a rare official framework from a frontier lab publicly articulating what superintelligence would require and how it might arrive — signaling that DeepMind is taking ASI timelines seriously enough to publish roadmap-level analysis.

## 6. Other · EU AI Act enforcement now live — fines and transparency mandates in effect

**Source:** [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/safer-and-more-transparent-ai-2026-08-02_en) · [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026) · [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/08/04/eu-ai-act-enforcement-ai-models/)

As of **August 2, 2026**, the EU AI Act's GPAI transparency obligations are enforceable. Any AI chatbot, voice agent, or interactive system must disclose to EU users at session start that they are interacting with AI. AI-generated or manipulated content (including deepfakes) must carry machine-readable detection labels. Fines reach up to €15M or 3% of global revenue. The AI Office's initial approach is "technical compliance dialogues" before formal proceedings.

**Why it matters:** This is the first major jurisdiction to enforce systemic AI transparency at scale, and US-based providers serving EU users are now exposed to fines. The delayed high-risk rules (pushed to December 2027) give enterprises time, but GPAI obligations are live today.
