## New Agent Tool

### 3. JetStream Launches Clearance — AI Zero Trust Reasoning Engine
**Source:** [Yahoo Finance / PR](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/jetstream-announces-clearance-ai-zero-170000815.html) · [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/jetstream-clearance-ai-agent-zero-trust-enterprise-september-2026/) · [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/feed/jetstream-launches-clearance-ai-zero-trust-engine-that-authorises-each-agent-action-before-execution)

JetStream announced Clearance on September 2, 2026 — a reasoning engine that evaluates every AI agent action against an approved design and decides whether it should run *before* it executes. Backed by $34M in seed funding from Redpoint and the CrowdStrike Falcon Fund, Clearance operates at the JetStream AI Gateway layer, mapping each request to the invoking agent, the approved policy, and the specific tool call.

**Why it matters:** Most agent security tools log what happened after the fact. Clearance moves authorization to pre-execution, blocking dangerous action sequences (such as exfiltration patterns) before they occur. As multi-agent deployments scale in enterprise environments, a dedicated authorization layer becomes infrastructure, not a nice-to-have.

## Research

### 7. Declarative Attention Cuts Attended Tokens by 52% Without Accuracy Loss
**Source:** [arXiv cs.AI — September 2026](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.AI/current)

A new paper introduces the Declarative Attention (DA) protocol, which lets language models explicitly manage their own attention span through generated reasoning. On Gemma-4-31B, DA reduced attended tokens by 52.0%; on Qwen-3.6-27B, by 31.1% — with no meaningful degradation in task accuracy.

**Why it matters:** Attention cost scales quadratically with sequence length; cutting attended tokens by half would have outsized impact on inference cost for long-context reasoning. A model-directed approach (rather than a fixed external sparsity pattern) adapts per-input, which is more robust than static attention approximations.

### 8. Random Attention Boosts vLLM Serving Throughput 32–43%
**Source:** [arXiv cs.LG — September 2026](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/current)

A September 2026 paper presents a Random Attention serving strategy for efficient LLM inference. The approach achieves reasoning accuracy comparable to signal-based attention selection while increasing serving throughput by 32–43% in vLLM deployments.

**Why it matters:** vLLM is the dominant open-source LLM serving runtime. A 32–43% throughput improvement with no accuracy regression — and no model retraining required — is immediately deployable and would materially reduce cost per token for self-hosted inference at scale.

### 10. California AI Transparency Act Now Operative
**Source:** [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative) · [OneTrust](https://www.onetrust.com/blog/california-ai-transparency-act-requirements-deadlines-and-compliance-steps/) · [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/new-obligations-under-the-california-ai-transparency-act-and-companion-chatbot-law-add-to-the-compliance-list)

California's AI Transparency Act (AB 853) became operative August 2, 2026. It applies to any publicly accessible generative AI system with more than one million monthly users. Covered providers must now offer a free AI content detection tool, support visible manifest disclosures on AI-generated content, and embed latent disclosures in AI-generated image, video, and audio. Penalties are $5,000 per violation.

**Why it matters:** With its 1M-user threshold, the Act covers virtually every major consumer GenAI product. As the first operative US state-level disclosure law for generative AI content, California sets a de facto national standard — companies that comply in California will apply the same tooling nationwide. The law's downstream flow-through obligation (extending requirements to third-party licensees) is the mechanism most likely to reshape how model providers structure their API terms.
