## New Model

### 1. Google Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash Reaches General Availability
**Source:** [AI Updates Today (November 2026)](https://llm-stats.com/llm-updates)

Google's Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash reached GA on November 5, becoming the first Omni model available on Google Cloud. It supports up to 15-second video clips with video output priced at ~$12.00 per 1M tokens (~$0.07/s at 720p). **Why it matters:** The GA milestone opens production-grade multimodal video generation to Google Cloud customers at scale for the first time.

## New Agent Tool

### 4. Microsoft APM — "npm for AI Agents"
**Source:** [AI Agents News — Week of November 8, 2026](https://aiagentstore.ai/ai-agent-news/this-week)

Microsoft released APM (Agent Package Manager) on November 7 under the MIT license. Modeled on npm, it provides dependency management and versioning built specifically for AI agent components. **Why it matters:** Standardized dependency management has been a gap in multi-agent development; APM gives teams a reproducible way to share and version agent modules.

---

### 5. Google ADK 2.14.0 — Experimental Features Graduate to Stable
**Source:** [AI Agents News — Week of November 8, 2026](https://aiagentstore.ai/ai-agent-news/this-week)

Google released Agent Development Kit 2.14.0 on November 7, graduating several experimental APIs to stable across its Python, TypeScript, Go, Java, and Kotlin SDKs. **Why it matters:** Stability promotions reduce churn for teams building production agents on Google's open-source toolkit and signal maturity in Google's agent platform strategy.

## Research

### 8. OSWorld Agent Benchmark: Success Rate Jumps from 12% to 66%
**Source:** [Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index](https://signaldrifts.substack.com/p/ai-research-papers-of-the-week3)

The Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index reports that AI agent performance on the OSWorld computer-task benchmark rose from roughly 12% to ~66% over the past year. **Why it matters:** The jump quantifies how rapidly agents are acquiring the ability to perform real computer tasks, a key metric for practical automation.

---

### 9. Backdoor Persistence Survives Post-Training — Anthropic/Berkeley/UIUC Paper
**Source:** [AI Research Papers of the Week](https://signaldrifts.substack.com/p/ai-research-papers-of-the-week3)

A joint paper from Anthropic, UC Berkeley, and UIUC demonstrates that poisoning a tiny fraction of pretraining data can embed a backdoor that survives RLHF and other post-training alignment procedures. **Why it matters:** The finding is a significant safety concern for supply-chain integrity: if training data is not carefully controlled, alignment techniques may not remove adversarially planted behaviors.
