### 1. Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2
**Source:** [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-fable-5-2-model-details-here/) · [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-fable-coding-upgrade/) · [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/?p=61724)

### 2. Google Gemini 4 Carbon Reaches General Availability
**Source:** [Google Blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/) · [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/google-gemini-4-carbon-is-now-available-for-complex-ai-tasks/) · [NewsBytesApp](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/google-launches-gemini-4-carbon-exclusively-for-ai-ultra-subscribers/tldr)

Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon to paying developers, enterprises, and AI Ultra subscribers on November 10, following a two-week restricted period for cybersecurity defenders. Carbon is Google's highest-tier frontier model, boasting more than double the secure-code success rate of Gemini 4 Argon on internal benchmarks and a headline "agent swarm" preview capability. Introductory pricing ($2/M input, $10/M output) runs through November 30 before doubling. **Why it matters:** Carbon puts Google directly in competition with the latest Anthropic and OpenAI frontier offerings, with cybersecurity and agentic work as the primary target use cases.

### 3. Meta Launches Muse Ember 1.0 — Cheapest Model in the Muse Family
**Source:** [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/meta-muse-ember-launch/) · [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/meta-muse-ember-smallest-model-ai-agents) · [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/computing/meta-launches-muse-ember-a-cheaper-ai-model-built-for-repetitive-tasks)

Meta released Muse Ember 1.0 on November 9–10 as the smallest and most cost-efficient Muse model ($0.10/M input, $0.50/M output). It carries a 1M-token context window, accepts text, image, audio, and video, and is designed to handle high-volume repetitive agent subtasks — tool calls, routing, extraction, and long-document parsing — while deferring planning to a larger model such as Muse Wonder. **Why it matters:** A cheap, capable execution model that pairs with a larger planner is the emerging pattern for multi-agent systems; Muse Ember validates and accelerates that pattern at Meta's scale.

### 4. ElevenLabs Ships Scribe v3 and Scribe v3 Live
**Source:** [ElevenLabs Blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/scribe-v3) · [RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/elevenlabs-scribe-v3-live-launch) · [Cut.pro](https://cut.pro/en/blog/elevenlabs-scribe-v3-transcription-2026)

ElevenLabs launched Scribe v3 (for recorded audio) and Scribe v3 Live (real-time streaming) on November 10. Scribe v3 cuts word-error rate by 27% across 102 languages versus v2, while the Live variant reduces errors by 43% versus Scribe v2 Realtime and adds real-time speaker diarisation — a first for the product. Both are live in ElevenAPI and the ElevenLabs app. **Why it matters:** High-accuracy, real-time multilingual transcription with speaker identification is a key dependency for voice agents; these improvements reduce a persistent bottleneck.

## New Agent Tool

### 5. IBM and Microsoft Announce "Imagine Enterprise" Agentic Platform
**Source:** [IBM Newsroom](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-11-10-ibm-and-microsoft-announce-a-new-ai-agent-platform-designed-for-industry-specific-transformation) · [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ibm-and-microsoft-announce-a-new-ai-agent-platform-designed-for-industry-specific-transformation-302934280.html)

On November 10, IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise — a jointly built platform for deploying, running, and governing fleets of AI agents for industry-specific work. IBM contributes a fleet of over 6,000 pre-built AI agents from IBM Consulting Advantage, while Microsoft provides enterprise identity, security, and Azure infrastructure. ## Research

### 6. arXiv: "AI Isn't Getting Dumber, We're Getting Pickier"
**Source:** [ArXiv AI Research Digest 2026-11-11](https://github.com/datnguyenquy94/news-radar/issues/1043)

A paper circulating in the November 11 arXiv digest argues that widely-reported degradation in LLM quality is largely an artefact of steadily rising evaluation standards rather than genuine model decline. The authors present empirical evidence that benchmark difficulty has increased faster than model performance, creating a misleading impression of regression. **Why it matters:** If confirmed, this reframes the ongoing "models are getting worse" discourse and shifts the focus toward calibrating evaluations rather than emergency retraining.

### 7. arXiv: ReTrace — Distilling Tool-Using Agents into Planner-Free Workflows
**Source:** [ArXiv AI Research Digest 2026-11-11](https://github.com/datnguyenquy94/news-radar/issues/1043)

ReTrace proposes a method for distilling the learned tool-use patterns of a full agent into compact, planner-free workflows that run faster and more predictably. The approach targets the latency and cost overhead of calling a large reasoning model for every step of a tool-using pipeline. **Why it matters:** Reducing inference cost for agentic workflows without sacrificing task-completion quality is an active research challenge; this paper offers a practical distillation path.

### 8. OpenAI Retiring AgentKit and Evals Platform on November 30
**Source:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-agentkit/) · [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/openai-launches-agentkit-to-transform-chatgpt-into-ai-development-platform/)

OpenAI updated the AgentKit page with a wind-down notice: the hosted Agent Builder and Evals products will be shut down on November 30, 2026. Developers are directed to migrate to the Agents SDK for code-based workflows and to bring their own evaluation tooling. The original AgentKit launched October 2025. **Why it matters:** Teams relying on hosted OpenAI Evals have two weeks to migrate; the move signals OpenAI's shift toward code-centric, SDK-driven agent tooling over no-code builders.
