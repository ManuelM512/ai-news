## [New Model] Shanghai AI Lab Ships Atria Dawn Preview — 744B Agentic MoE Under MIT

Source: https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/atria-releases-atria-dawn-preview-121900267.html

Summary: Shanghai AI Laboratory released Atria Dawn Preview, a 744B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model built on the GLM-5.2 foundation and designed for long-horizon research and engineering agents. It targets the full scientific loop — problem analysis, tool use, code execution, experiment running, result analysis, and failure recovery — within a 256K context window. Weights (full-precision and FP8) are available on Hugging Face under the MIT license. Launch benchmarks report 53.8 on AutomationBench, 86.5 on CyberGym, and 77.0 on BFCL v4.

Why it matters: At 744B parameters with native tool-use tuning and a fully open license, Atria Dawn Preview is one of the most capable open-weight agentic models yet released. It directly challenges proprietary frontier models on autonomous research tasks and sets a new bar for what open-source labs can self-host.

## [New Agent Tool] OpenAI Agents API Enters Public Beta

Source: https://openai.com/index/introducing-the-agents-api/

Summary: OpenAI opened its Agents API to all developers in public beta, bringing the orchestration harness that powers Codex to third-party builders. The API handles long-running sessions, automatic context compaction, and compute environment selection — developers can route agents to OpenAI-hosted sandboxes or partner infrastructure from Blaxel, Cloudflare, Daytona, DigitalOcean, E2B, Modal, Oracle, Runloop, and Vercel. Billing is usage-based (tokens + tools only) with no API surcharge.

Why it matters: Managed agent orchestration as a commodity primitive — rather than something each team builds themselves — could substantially lower the barrier to shipping production agentic applications. The partner compute roster also signals OpenAI is building a platform ecosystem around agents, not just a model API.

## [New Model] Z.ai Releases GLM-5.3-Flash: First Natively Multimodal GLM-5 Model

Source: https://github.com/zai-org/GLM-5

Summary: Z.ai (formerly Zhipu AI) shipped GLM-5.3-Flash, a 320B-parameter MoE model (18B active) that is the first in the GLM-5 series to handle text, image, and video inputs natively. It carries a 1M-token context window, self-reported DeepSWE score of 63.4 (up from GLM-5.2's 46.2), and is available as open weights on Hugging Face under the MIT license at $0.15/$0.03 cached per 1M tokens.

Why it matters: GLM-5.3-Flash extends the competitive open-source multimodal field with a lean activated-parameter count that makes it efficient to serve, while the MIT license enables commercial deployment without restrictions. The strong coding benchmark improvement over its predecessor widens the gap between GLM-5 and earlier open-weight alternatives.

## [Other] Linux Foundation Takes Governance of TRACE — Open Standard for AI Runtime Attestation

Source: https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-welcomes-trace-to-advance-verifiable-runtime-evidence-for-ai-workloads

Summary: The Linux Foundation announced governance of TRACE (Trust, Runtime Attestation and Compliance Evidence), an open specification for hardware-attested, cryptographically verifiable records of AI agent execution. Originally contributed by OPAQUE and developed with AMD, Anthropic, Intel, Microsoft, and NVIDIA, TRACE generates "Trust Records" that capture runtime environment, executed software, data classifications, and enforced policies — all rooted in silicon attestation. It recorded 135,000 PyPI downloads within 10 weeks of its June 2026 debut.

Why it matters: As AI agents make consequential decisions, regulators and enterprises increasingly need verifiable audit trails of what an agent did, not just what it was told to do. TRACE addresses this with hardware-level guarantees rather than software promises, and Linux Foundation governance gives it the vendor-neutral credibility needed for widespread adoption.

## [Research] Cognition's Devin AI Factors RSA-260 — Largest Number Factored in 35 Years

Source: https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-launches-agents-api-in-public-beta-for-developers-93CH-2692209

Summary: Cognition engineer Eric Lu used Devin, Cognition's software engineering AI agent, to develop a GPU-accelerated implementation of the General Number Field Sieve (GNFS) and factor RSA-260, a 260-digit (862-bit) semiprime that had resisted factoring since the 1991 RSA Factoring Challenge. Devin rebuilt the square-root phase three times, ultimately using GPU-accelerated NTT multiplication to finish in 88 minutes. The total compute was approximately 4,900 GPU-days.

Why it matters: This is both a milestone in computational number theory — replacing RSA-250 (2020) as the largest GNFS factorization — and a concrete demonstration of what a software engineering agent can accomplish on a months-long, highly technical research task. The result does not threaten modern 2048-bit RSA but does push the frontier of what AI-assisted engineering can tackle autonomously.

## [Research] RAG Safety and Architecture Research Surges on arXiv (September 18)

Source: https://github.com/jyyang621/DailyArXiv/issues/414

Summary: The September 18 arXiv CS batch included a concentrated burst of RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) papers, including RAG-Safety-Bench (reliable safety evaluation for RAG-augmented LLMs), RECTIFY (an interactive workbench for post-evaluation RAG diagnosis and repair), and multiple studies on dynamic retriever selection, context-aware graph RAG vs. standard RAG, and privacy risks in RAG systems. Several papers specifically focus on closing the gap between synthetic benchmark queries and real-world retrieval distributions.

Why it matters: The volume and breadth of RAG safety and architecture papers signals the field is moving from "does RAG work?" to "how do we make RAG reliable, secure, and trustworthy at scale?" — a shift that mirrors what happened with standard model safety research two to three years earlier, and that will inform enterprise deployment standards.

## [Other] OpenAI Economic Research: Workers Integrating AI Into Non-Traditional Monthly Workflows

Source: https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/openai-launches-agents-api-in-public-beta-for-developers-93CH-2692209

Summary: OpenAI's Economic Research Team released the second installment of its "Work at the Frontier" series, finding that workers have moved beyond initial cross-occupational AI experimentation and are now integrating non-traditional tasks into recurring monthly workflows. The study shows a structural evolution: AI is no longer a novelty tool tried once but is becoming embedded in regular job routines across occupations.

Why it matters: Longitudinal behavioral data on how AI adoption actually evolves in the workplace is rare. This report offers quantitative signal that habit formation around AI tools is real and accelerating, with implications for workforce planning, product design, and the timeline of productivity impacts at the economy level.
